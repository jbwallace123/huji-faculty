Kempner Institute x The Edmond & Lily Safra Center for Brain Sciences NeuroAI Collaboration

Recent advances in machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) are transforming science, technology, medicine, and the global economy. Yet, despite impressive progress, current AI systems still fall short of the flexibility, efficiency, and robustness of human intelligence.

Against this backdrop, a new interdisciplinary research domain—NeuroAI—has emerged, dedicated to the systematic integration of neuroscience and artificial intelligence. NeuroAI seeks to foster a two-way exchange of ideas, methods, and research questions that advances both our understanding of the brain and the design of intelligent machines.

The NeuroAI Initiative

Building on deep expertise in NeuroAI and an already warm relationship, the Kempner Institute for the Study of Natural and Artificial Intelligence at Harvard University and the Hebrew University of Jerusalem (HUJI) have established a joint NeuroAI initiative focused on the fundamental connections between natural and artificial intelligence.

The Kempner Institute brings together experts in ML and AI, neuroscience, cognitive psychology, and related fields to address critical questions in intelligence. It has built a large and collaborative community of cross-disciplinary researchers at all stages of training, supported by shared dry-lab research space, access to world-class computational resources, and support from a dedicated team of expert research software engineers.

The Hebrew University of Jerusalem is a natural collaborator in this endeavor. HUJI is a world-leading center of computational neuroscience, hosted in the Edmond and Lily Safra Center for Brain Sciences (ELSC). It is home to a distinguished AI community in the School of Computer Science and Engineering, as well as a strong Cognitive Science department.

Strong foundations for collaboration already exist between the Kempner Institute and Israeli scientists working in NeuroAI, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. These include the appointment of Dr. Haim Sompolinsky—who holds joint affiliations at Harvard University and the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and was the first Associate Faculty member of the Kempner Institute—the presence of multiple Kempner Research Fellows who completed their graduate training in Israel, and the appointment of two Israeli faculty members as Kempner Visiting Professors.

Vision

To create a vibrant and sustained interchange between the two academic communities, the NeuroAI Initiative is launching an initial set of interactive activities designed to promote the exchange of ideas, data, and results, and to identify concrete, high-impact projects for future collaborative research. These activities include:

  • Short-term visits. A select number of graduate students, postdoctoral fellows, and faculty members will be invited to visit the partner institution for periods of one to three months, embedded within research groups with complementary expertise.
  • Sabbaticals. One to two principal investigators will be invited to spend extended sabbatical periods—up to one year—at the partner institution, enabling deep immersion in new methodologies and the development of sustained collaborative research programs.
  • Workshops. A series of joint workshops will serve as focal points for brainstorming, sharing results, and shaping the emerging research agenda across the two institutions.

With additional support, the Kempner Institute and HUJI will be able to pursue a deeper and more advanced collaboration, accelerating research findings and strengthening interactions. In addition to allowing more students, faculty and fellows to participate in international exchanges and visits, the NeuroAI Initiative would be able to expand to include:

  • Advanced training in NeuroAI. Joint courses, summer schools, focused tutorials, and co-mentored research projects to train a new generation of scientists fluent in both neuroscience and AI, and comfortable working across experimental, theoretical, and computational approaches.
  • Seed funding for collaborative research. A joint seed-funding program to support high-risk, high-reward NeuroAI projects involving investigators from both institutions. These pilot grants would enable teams to generate preliminary results and proof-of-concept studies, positioning them for competitive external funding.
  • Access to compute resources. Access to large-scale computational resources has become a critical bottleneck in NeuroAI research. The Kempner Institute's state-of-the-art compute infrastructure, together with seed funding, could be leveraged to support joint collaborative research projects involving HUJI and Kempner researchers at scale.
